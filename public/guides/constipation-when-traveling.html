Constipation When Traveling: How to Prevent It

By Mark Laurence E. Medina, founder and editor · Updated November 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Educational information, not medical advice. This guide is general information for healthy adults and is not reviewed for your personal situation. Talk with a clinician about symptoms that are new, severe, or persistent. When to see a doctor

Travel constipation is very common. New time zones, long hours sitting, less water, different food and unfamiliar bathrooms all disrupt your normal rhythm. The good news: a little planning before, during and after your trip prevents most of it.

Why travel causes constipation

  • Your routine changes. Different wake-up and meal times shift your bowel’s natural rhythm.
  • You drink less. Many people drink less on travel days to avoid bathroom stops, and air cabins are dry.
  • You sit for hours in cars, planes and trains, with less movement than usual.
  • Food is different and often lower in fiber: white bread, snacks and restaurant meals.
  • You hold it in because the bathroom is busy, unfamiliar or not clean. Ignoring the urge is one of the most common triggers for constipation.

Before you go

  • Keep your fiber steady in the days before travel instead of eating less.
  • Pack portable fiber: kiwifruit, prunes, an apple, whole-grain crackers, nuts or oat bars. Check the customs rules for fresh fruit if you are flying abroad.
  • Bring an empty refillable water bottle through airport security.
  • If you usually take a fiber supplement, pack it. Starting a brand-new supplement the day you fly is not a good idea, because it can cause gas.

During the trip

  1. Drink regularly. Water is best. Go easy on alcohol, which the NHS advises avoiding when constipated.
  2. Move every hour or two. Walk the aisle on a plane, or stop and stretch on a road trip.
  3. Keep breakfast as normal as you can, ideally with fruit, oats or whole grains. Hotel buffets usually have options.
  4. Make time for the toilet after breakfast, as you would at home. See our morning routine guide.
  5. Do not hold it in. When you feel the urge, go when you reasonably can.
  6. Raise your feet. If there is no footstool, a small bin or your backpack can raise your knees above your hips.

Crossing time zones

After a long flight, try to switch to local meal and sleep times as quickly as possible. A regular breakfast at the new local time helps reset your bowel’s rhythm along with your sleep. Expect a few days of irregularity, especially after long eastbound flights.

When you get home

Return to your usual meals, fluids and routine. Most travel constipation settles within a few days. If it does not, look at fiber and fluids first. Our guides on foods that help constipation and water and constipation are a good place to start. A pharmacist can recommend a short-term laxative if you need one.

When to see a doctor

Get medical advice, at home or abroad, if you have:

  • Severe belly pain, vomiting, or you cannot pass stool or gas at all (seek urgent care)
  • Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools
  • Calf pain or swelling after a long flight or drive (this needs urgent attention, separate from constipation)
  • Constipation that lasts more than a week or two after you get home
