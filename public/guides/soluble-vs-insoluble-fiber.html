Soluble vs Insoluble Fiber: What Is the Difference?

By Mark Laurence E. Medina, founder and editor · Updated November 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Educational information, not medical advice. This guide is general information for healthy adults and is not reviewed for your personal situation. Talk with a clinician about symptoms that are new, severe, or persistent. When to see a doctor

Soluble fiber dissolves in water and forms a soft gel. Insoluble fiber does not dissolve and adds bulk. Both help digestion in different ways, and most plant foods contain some of each. For constipation, a mix works best, and the gel-forming fiber psyllium has some of the strongest evidence.

Side by side

Soluble fiberInsoluble fiber
What it doesAbsorbs water and forms a gel, which softens stoolAdds bulk and helps stool move along
Good sourcesOats, barley, beans, lentils, apples, citrus, chia, psylliumWheat bran, whole-wheat bread, brown rice, nuts, vegetable and fruit skins
Other benefitsCan help lower LDL cholesterol and steady blood sugarSupports regularity; many are partly fermented by gut bacteria
Watch out forSome types cause gas while your gut adjustsLarge amounts of bran without enough fluid can make stools harder

Which is better for constipation?

Both help, and you do not need to count each type separately. A few practical points from the evidence:

  • Psyllium is a soluble, gel-forming fiber supplement. The 2025 British Dietetic Association guidelines support trying fiber supplements such as psyllium for constipation, at an effective dose for at least four weeks.
  • Wheat bran and other insoluble fibers add bulk. The NHS suggests adding wheat bran, oats or linseed if you are constipated.
  • Whole foods give you both. Kiwifruit, prunes, rye bread, beans and oats each contain a mix of fiber types. See foods that help constipation.

If you have irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), soluble fiber such as psyllium is usually better tolerated than insoluble bran, which can make bloating worse for some people. Ask a dietitian for advice that fits your situation.

How to get a good mix every day

  • Breakfast: oats (soluble) topped with berries and a spoon of ground flaxseed (both types)
  • Lunch: whole-grain or rye bread (mostly insoluble) with a bean or lentil soup (soluble)
  • Snack: an apple or two kiwifruit with the skin (both types)
  • Dinner: brown rice or whole-wheat pasta with plenty of vegetables (mostly insoluble)

Most adults need about 25 to 34 grams of fiber a day in total. Build up by around 5 grams every few days and drink enough fluid, because fiber needs water to work. See how much fiber you need a day for targets and a food table.

Taking a fiber supplement? Take it with a full glass of water, start with a small dose, and leave a gap of about two hours between it and any medicines, since fiber can affect how some medicines are absorbed. Check with a pharmacist if you are unsure.

When to see a doctor

More fiber is not the answer to every bowel problem. Contact a doctor if you notice:

  • Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Severe or persistent belly pain or bloating
  • A lasting change in your bowel habits
  • Constipation that does not improve after a few weeks of diet changes

If you have a diagnosed bowel condition or a history of bowel obstruction, ask your clinician what type and amount of fiber is safe for you. Read more in Constipation: when to see a doctor.
