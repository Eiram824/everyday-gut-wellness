Fiber

How Much Fiber Do You Need a Day?

By Mark Laurence E. Medina, founder and editor · Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Educational information, not medical advice. This guide is general information for healthy adults and is not reviewed for your personal situation. Talk with a clinician about symptoms that are new, severe, or persistent. When to see a doctor

Most adults need roughly 25 to 34 grams of fiber a day, yet most of us eat about half that. The good news: a few everyday swaps close most of the gap, as long as you add fiber gradually and drink enough fluid alongside it.

Daily fiber targets for adults

Official targets differ a little by country, but they point in the same direction:

WhoDaily targetSource
US women, 19–5025–28 gDietary Guidelines for Americans
US men, 19–5031–34 gDietary Guidelines for Americans
US adults 51+22–28 gDietary Guidelines for Americans
UK adults30 gNHS / Scientific Advisory Committee on Nutrition

The US guidance works out to about 14 grams for every 1,000 calories you eat, which is why the number is higher for people who eat more. Surveys in both countries show the average adult falls well short, so if you are not counting, you are probably below target too.

Why fiber matters for regular digestion

Fiber is the part of plant foods your body cannot fully digest. That is exactly what makes it useful:

  • Soluble fiber (oats, beans, lentils, apples, chia) absorbs water and forms a soft gel, which helps keep stool soft.
  • Insoluble fiber (whole grains, wheat bran, nuts, vegetable skins) adds bulk, which helps stool move along.
  • Many fibers also feed the helpful bacteria in your large intestine.

Most whole plant foods contain both types, so variety matters more than chasing one “best” fiber.

What 30 grams can look like in one day

  • Breakfast: 1 cup oatmeal with ½ cup raspberries and 1 tablespoon chia (about 12 g)
  • Lunch: whole-wheat sandwich plus an apple (about 9 g)
  • Dinner: ½ cup lentils or beans in a soup, curry, or salad with a side of broccoli (about 12 g)

How to add fiber without bloating

A sudden jump in fiber is the most common reason people give up. Gas and bloating usually ease as your gut adjusts, so go slowly:

  1. Add about 5 grams at a time. One extra serving from the table above is plenty to start.
  2. Hold each step for 3–7 days before adding the next, until you reach your target over a few weeks.
  3. Drink fluid alongside it. Fiber works by holding water. Without enough fluid, more fiber can make stools harder, not softer.
  4. Spread it out across meals instead of one very high-fiber meal.
  5. Rotate sources – beans one day, berries or seeds the next – to get a mix of fiber types.

What about fiber supplements?

Food first is the usual advice, because whole foods bring vitamins, minerals, and a mix of fiber types. Some people still use a supplement such as psyllium. If you do, start with a small dose, drink a full glass of water with it, and check with a pharmacist or doctor first if you take regular medicines, since fiber supplements can affect how some are absorbed.

When to see a doctor

Fiber helps with everyday irregularity, but it is not a treatment for every bowel problem. Contact a doctor if you notice:

  • Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Severe or persistent belly pain, or pain that wakes you at night
  • A lasting change in your bowel habits, especially if you are over 50
  • Constipation that does not improve after a few weeks of diet changes

If you have a diagnosed condition such as IBS, inflammatory bowel disease, or a past bowel obstruction, ask your clinician how much and what type of fiber is right for you. Read more in Constipation: when to see a doctor.
