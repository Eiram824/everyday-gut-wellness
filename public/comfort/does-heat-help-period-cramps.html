Period cramps

Does Heat Help Period Cramps?

By Mark Laurence E. Medina, founder and editor · Updated November 24, 2026 · 3 min read

Educational information, not medical advice. This guide is general information for healthy adults and is not reviewed for your personal situation. Talk with a clinician about symptoms that are new, severe, or persistent. When to see a doctor

Short answer: yes, for many people. Studies of heat for period cramps show less pain than no heat, and in some trials about the same relief as a standard pain reliever. It is not a cure, and it does not fix cramps caused by another condition, but it is one of the safest things you can try at home.

What the studies show

A 2018 systematic review pooled six randomized trials of heat therapy for primary dysmenorrhea, the common kind of period pain with no other cause. Compared with an unheated patch or no treatment, heat reduced pain scores. In the trials that compared heat with anti-inflammatory pain relievers, the two gave similar relief. The authors noted that the trials were small and of limited quality, so larger studies are still needed.

The NHS also lists a heat pad or hot water bottle on your tummy as a first self-help step for period pain, alongside pain relievers, exercise and warm baths.

Why warmth may help

Researchers think heat helps in a few ways at once: it relaxes tense muscles, increases blood flow to the area, and changes how pain signals are felt, so the cramping feels less intense. Many people also simply find warmth calming, which matters when pain makes you tense.

How to use heat for cramps

QuestionPractical answer
How warm?Comfortably warm, never hot. Several trials used gentle, continuous heat around 39 °C (about 102 °F).
How long?Low heat can be used for longer with breaks. With stronger heat, use 15 to 20 minutes at a time and let your skin cool between sessions.
Where?The lower belly, just above the pubic bone, or the lower back.
When?As soon as cramps start. It is easier to keep pain low than to bring it down.
With a pain reliever?Yes, heat can be combined with ibuprofen or paracetamol if those are safe for you.

Types of heat, compared

  • Hot water bottle: cheap and soothing, but cools within an hour and can leak or scald if overfilled. Use warm, not boiling, water and a cover.
  • Microwavable heat pack: easy to reheat, but heats unevenly, so shake it and test it on your arm first.
  • Stick-on heat patch: low, steady heat for hours under clothing. Never use one on bare skin unless the pack says you can, and do not sleep with one.
  • Electric heating pad: steady heat at home. Use the lowest setting that helps and a pad with automatic shut-off.
  • Wearable heat belt: rechargeable and hands-free, so you can keep moving, which helps too.
  • Warm bath or shower: whole-body warmth and relaxation.

Whatever you use, read how to use a heating pad safely first. Burns from heat products are more common than people think.

When heat is not enough

Heat works best for ordinary period cramps. If your pain is severe, getting worse over time, or comes with heavy bleeding or pain during sex, heat may hide a problem that needs treatment, such as endometriosis or fibroids. Our guide to period cramps relief at home covers the other steps that help.

When to see a doctor

  • Cramps that stop you from doing normal daily things
  • Pain that heat and pain relievers do not help after two or three cycles
  • Cramps that are new or much worse than before
  • Very heavy periods, bleeding between periods or after sex, or pain during sex

Get urgent care for sudden severe belly pain, pain with a fever, fainting, or severe pain if you could be pregnant.

Sources
