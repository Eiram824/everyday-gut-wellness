Bloating

How to Relieve Bloating: Quick Fixes and Everyday Habits

By Mark Laurence E. Medina, founder and editor · Updated November 24, 2026 · 3 min read

Educational information, not medical advice. This guide is general information for healthy adults and is not reviewed for your personal situation. Talk with a clinician about symptoms that are new, severe, or persistent. When to see a doctor

Bloating is that tight, full, swollen feeling in your belly, sometimes with visible swelling by the end of the day. It is very common and usually harmless. Most bloating comes from gas, constipation, eating patterns, or your period, and simple changes help most people. Here is what to try now, and what to change over time.

Common causes of bloating

  • Swallowed air: eating quickly, talking while eating, chewing gum, fizzy drinks and drinking through a straw.
  • Gas from food: beans, lentils, onions, cabbage, broccoli, Brussels sprouts and some sweeteners (such as sorbitol) ferment in the gut and make gas.
  • Constipation: stool that moves slowly makes you feel full and heavy. See foods that help constipation.
  • Your period: hormone changes before and during a period commonly cause water retention and bloating.
  • Food intolerances and IBS: lactose intolerance, coeliac disease and irritable bowel syndrome often show up as bloating.
  • Adding fiber too fast: fiber is good for you, but a sudden jump causes gas. Our fiber guide shows how to increase it gently.

What helps right now

  • Take a 10 to 15 minute walk. Gentle movement helps gas move through and out of the gut, and research shows physical activity speeds up gas clearance.
  • Try gentle belly massage. With light pressure, move your hand in slow clockwise circles, following the path of the large bowel: up the right side, across, and down the left.
  • Use warmth. Many people find a warm compress or heat belt on the belly relaxing when they feel tight and crampy. Follow our heat safety rules.
  • Change position. Lying on your left side, or a gentle child’s pose, helps some people pass trapped wind.
  • Loosen tight waistbands and sip a warm, non-fizzy drink.

Everyday habits that prevent bloating

HabitWhy it helps
Eat slowly and chew wellLess swallowed air, easier digestion
Smaller, regular mealsLarge meals stretch the stomach and slow things down
Cut back on fizzy drinks and gumBoth add air to your gut
Increase fiber slowly, with fluidsKeeps you regular without a sudden gas spike
Move every dayActivity keeps the bowel moving
Keep a short food and symptom diaryHelps you and your doctor spot triggers

If certain foods clearly trigger you, you do not have to cut out whole food groups on your own. A doctor or registered dietitian can guide you through a structured approach, such as a low-FODMAP trial for IBS, without risking your nutrition.

Bloating around your period

Premenstrual bloating usually peaks in the days before bleeding and eases once your period starts. Regular meals, less salty processed food, staying active and warmth on the belly help many people feel more comfortable. If cramps come with it, see period cramps relief at home.

When to see a doctor

See a doctor if bloating:

  • Keeps coming back, or lasts most days for three weeks or more
  • Comes with weight loss you cannot explain, or feeling full quickly
  • Comes with blood in your stool, black stools, or a lasting change in bowel habits
  • Comes with a lump in your belly, or pain that does not go away
  • Is new and persistent after age 50

Persistent bloating can be a sign of a condition such as coeliac disease, and, in women, it is one of the symptoms doctors check for ovarian cancer. That is rare, but it is why lasting bloating should always be checked. Get urgent care for severe belly pain, vomiting, or if you cannot pass stool or gas.
